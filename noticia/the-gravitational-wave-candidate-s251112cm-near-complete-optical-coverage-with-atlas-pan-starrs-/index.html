Texto completo

On 2025 November 12, the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA network detected S251112cm, a compact binary merger candidate with a false alarm rate of one per four years at a luminosity distance of $93 \pm 27$ Mpc. On 2025 November 12, the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA (LVK) network detected S251112cm, a compact binary merger candidate with a false alarm rate of one per four years at a luminosity distance of $93 \pm 27$ Mpc.

The probability that the lighter component's mass lies below 1 $M_\odot$ is >99%, while the probability that either component lies at 1-3 $M_\odot$ is only 8%, leaving the nature of the progenitor system unclear. In the North, Pan-STARRS imaged 1084 deg$^2$ (65%) within 7 days to a median depth of 21.9 mag and ATLAS 1545 deg$^2$ (87%) within 14 days to 19.3 mag across both hemispheres.

In the South, Rubin covered 849 deg$^2$ (36%) 2-10 days post-merger to median depths of 24.5 ($g$) and 23.5 ($i$) mag. They reported 173, 93 and 67 candidates respectively (269 unique), which we assess against AT 2017gfo-like kilonova and SN 1998bw-like broad-lined Type Ic supernova models using prior activity, host luminosity distance, photometric evolution and absolute magnitude.

Eight survive, mainly for lack of distance information. Six of these lie in hosts fainter than any in a volume-limited comparison sample, suggesting they are background sources.

AT 2025aimy declines too slowly for the AT 2017gfo-like model, and a VLT host spectrum places AT 2025alqe outside the LVK volume, leaving no convincing counterpart candidates. Pan-STARRS and Rubin together exclude both models in $\sim$80% of the 90% localisation volume: the deepest wide-area constraint on a subsolar-mass merger to date and, to our knowledge, the most complete optical coverage of a significant subsolar-mass candidate.
