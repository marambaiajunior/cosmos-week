We present NEMESIS II, a blind transit search of nearby M-dwarfs in TESS Primary Mission full-frame images (Sectors 1-26), including stars lacking 2-minute observations.

Using the open-source NEMESIS pipeline, we extracted, detrended, and searched 213, 658 single-sector light curves of 113, 047 M-dwarfs within 100 pc, yielding 23, 339 threshold crossing events. Automated LEO-Vetter vetting, applied identically to the survey and its injection-recovery and orbit-scrambling controls, retains 558 detections corresponding to 525 distinct signals with converged transit fits and periods of 0.51-9.10 days.

Of these, 482 lie on stars without TOI, CTOI, or NASA Exoplanet Archive transiting-planet entries. Assuming an astrophysical signal, TRICERATOPS favors a planetary origin for 262 of 525 candidates (232 new), which we classify as planet candidates.

It also excludes resolved-neighbor eclipses for 105. Known planets and TOI candidates survive vetting at 1.01 +/- 0.07 of the injection-predicted rate.

Correcting for each light curve's sensitivity, false alarms, and astrophysical false positives, we infer 0.81 +/- 0.44 planets per star for periods between 1.0-10.0 days and planet radii between 0.5-11.0 Earth radii. This is a lower limit, because our injections recover few or no planets near and below 1 Earth radii and assume central transits.

Light curves, catalogs, vetting reports, and injection-recovery results will be released as a High Level Science Product at MAST upon publication.
