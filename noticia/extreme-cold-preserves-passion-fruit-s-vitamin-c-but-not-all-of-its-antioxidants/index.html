The results reveal two sides of the same technology: While vitamin C remained virtually intact, other components linked to the fruit's antioxidant capacity dropped significantly. The research is published in the International Journal of Food Properties.

Then came the thermal shock: The samples were immersed in liquid nitrogen at -196 °C (-321°F) until they reached a temperature of -40 °C (-40°F) at their core. One of the most relevant results emerged when the team measured ascorbic acid.

When they analyzed the pulp's total antioxidant capacity, the researchers found a 23.6% decrease after the cryogenic treatment. The differences were even greater for two specific groups of bioactive compounds: Total phenolics fell by 40.3% and flavonoids by 35.7%.
