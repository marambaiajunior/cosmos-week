Texto completo

Power electronic devices help control the flow of electricity and convert it into forms needed by different machines, such as electric vehicles, renewable energy systems and industrial equipment. This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies.

To investigate these properties, researchers at Hokkaido University and Kyoto University prepared thin films of alpha-gallium oxide using a technique called mist chemical vapor deposition. The study was published in ACS Applied Electronic Materials.

The team first measured the electrical properties of the films, including electron concentration and thermopower, which describes the voltage generated per unit temperature difference across a material. They found that this was approximately 40% of a free electron's mass, higher than the theoretically predicted value of 25%.

The team then measured how efficiently heat travels through the films. They found a thermal conductivity of approximately 19 watts per meter-kelvin.

Together, these results show that adding germanium can improve electron movement without significantly changing heat transfer through the material. Hiromichi Ohta et al, Experimental Determination of Carrier Effective Mass and Cross-plane Thermal Conductivity in Mist-CVD-Grown Ge-Doped α-Ga 2 O 3 Epitaxial Films, ACS Applied Electronic Materials (2026).
