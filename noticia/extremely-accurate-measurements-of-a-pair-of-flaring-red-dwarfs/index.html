Extremely Accurate Measurements of a Pair of Flaring Red Dwarfs

Astronomers used the Very Large Baseline Array of radio telescopes to measure a pair of active red dwarfs with extreme precision.

Texto completo

Astronomers used the Very Large Baseline Array of radio telescopes to measure a pair of active red dwarfs with extreme precision. One of the stars is an extreme type of red dwarf called a Complex Periodic Variable.

A team of astronomers used that to their advantage to take precision measurements of a binary pair of M dwarfs about 59 million light years from Earth. They're called Complex Periodic Variable (CPV) stars, and they're very young, between about 10 and 100 million years old.

The measurments of DG CVn are in new research in Astronomy and Astrophysics titled " Accurate dynamical mass and orbit determination of the M dwarf binary DG CVn using astrometric very long baseline interferometry. It's noteworthy that this is the first mass measurement of a system with a CPV star.

The astronomers' results are based on observations that spanned 18 years. Observations with the National Science Foundation’s Very Long Baseline Array (VLBA) between 2023 and 2025 were especially important in their precise results.

The 10 dishes work as an interferometer, and the VLBA has the longest baseline in the world, up to 8, 611 km long. A unique and accurate determination of the 1647.647 ± 0.027 day orbit, with a parallax distance of 18.1851 ± 0.0033 pc (about 59 light years), is presented.

The dynamical masses were estimated with a precision of 0.16% from the sampled posteriors. " The pair of stars orbit each other every 4.511 years on an elongated elliptical orbit.
