A precision T-cell therapy stalls treatment-resistant cancers in clinical trial

Experimental drug invika halts progression of refractory tumors by selectively stimulating tumor-fighting T cells.

Texto completo

Experimental drug invika halts progression of refractory tumors by selectively stimulating tumor-fighting T cells. D, clinical director at the Center for Cancer Research (CCR), part of NIH’s National Cancer Institute (NCI).

This new study describes the next step for invika, detailing the results of phase 1 and initial phase 2 experiments in humans. In phase 1, they treated study participants with varying dosages of invika every two weeks to evaluate the drug’s safety and determine the optimal dose.

After identifying the lowest dose at which desired effects peaked, the researchers advanced to phase 2, treating participants at this optimal dose. Of the 27 participants treated with the optimal dose across both phases, disease did not progress in 16 (59.3%) of them.

Four (14.8%) participants experienced greater than 30% decreases in the size of targeted tumors, two of whom had metastatic colorectal cancer with tumors of a notoriously treatment-resistant type and were receiving invika as their seventh-line therapy. Another responsive participant had stage 4 non-small cell lung cancer, again with tumors that had repelled multiple therapies including ICB.
