This study, published in the journal ISME Host Microbe, offers clues to which functions remain in symbiotic bacteria living closely with their host.

Understanding this symbiosis is of great interest to researchers and marine biologists, both from an evolutionary perspective and because in some areas of the deep sea, sea cucumbers account for more than 90% of the biomass of large bottom-dwelling animals. What we found was that in body-wall samples from the three sea pig species, one bacterium accounted for more than 70% of bacterial 16S rRNA gene sequences (a genetic marker used in identifying bacteria), so it truly dominated the body wall," explained Yoshida.
