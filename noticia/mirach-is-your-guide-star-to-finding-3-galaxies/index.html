Mirach in the constellation Andromeda helps stargazers locate the Andromeda Galaxy, the Triangulum Galaxy, and "Mirach's Ghost. " The post Mirach is your guide star to finding 3 galaxies first appeared on EarthSky. You can find the Andromeda galaxy (M31), the large spiral galaxy next door to our Milky Way, by star-hopping with Mirach.

Keep up with all phases of the moon every night of the year. It shines with about 1, 900 times our sun’s total brightness.

Even if the moon is out or under skies with moderate light pollution (like the suburbs), you can see it through binoculars. Even so, it can still be a challenge to find M33 with binoculars and small telescopes.

M31 is easy to find on a dark night, from a rural location. Finding Mirach’s Ghost (NGC 404) When you look at Mirach, you’re looking almost exactly in the direction of the galaxy labeled NGC 404 but known affectionately among astronomers as Mirach’s Ghost.

That’s because the remote, fuzzy galaxy lies right next to the moderately bright star, just 1/10 degree from Mirach. A full moon is 1/2 degree wide, so you can see that’s very close.

Many amateur astronomers try to spot it with their small telescopes, but due to its proximity to the brighter 2nd-magnitude Mirach, seeing the galaxy is not easy.
