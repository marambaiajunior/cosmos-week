Feast Your Eyes on Uranus at Opposition This Thanksgiving 2026

The outer planets are ever so slowly starting to sneak back into the evening sky this month.

Texto completo

The outer planets are ever so slowly starting to sneak back into the evening sky this month. First, Saturn crossed this mark last month on October 4th, then Neptune followed suit on September 26th.

Shout out to the Full November Beaver Moon nearby on the same date as opposition 2026, fresh off of occulting the Pleiades for the Arctic on November 24th. Uranus reached aphelion 20 Astronomical Units (AU) from the Sun in 2009, and is now inbound toward perihelion 18.3 AU distant in 2050.

In 2033, Uranus will pass the point in Taurus where it was discovered by Sir William Herschel on March 13th, 1781, between the horns of the Bull very near the constellation's border with Gemini. Credit: Dave Dickinson/Starry Night Uranus reached its southernmost point in the sky in 2007, and crossed the celestial equator multiple times from 2011-2012.

The Moon won’t resume occulting the planet Uranus again until a cycle starting on September 29th, 2029. The Moon occults the Pleiades on November 24th.

In fact, that’s exactly what Herschel thought he’d first discovered, back on that fortuitous night in 1781. Uranus is tipped over 98 degrees (prograde) on its side, the result of some ancient encounter with another large body early in the solar system’s history.

Though Uranus has 29 known moons, only five are within potential reach of amateur telescopes, with +13.9 magnitude Titania being the brightest.
