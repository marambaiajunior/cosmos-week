A study published in Frontiers in Sustainable Food Systems reveals that, although partnerships and institutional collaboration feature prominently in climate-oriented agricultural research, their relationship with the scaling of innovations is rarely explicitly documented.

By The Alliance of Bioversity International and the International Center for Tropical Agriculture This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies. To this end, Deissy Martínez-Barón, a researcher at the International Center for Tropical Agriculture (CIAT), currently part of the Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT, and at the University of Copenhagen.

And Steven Dean Prager, from the Gates Foundation, analyzed 273 results reports covering 261 projects across 12 CGIAR research programs, reported between 2018 and 2021 under the former CGIAR Research Program on Climate Change, Agriculture and Food Security (CCAFS).

To identify these patterns, the researchers used a model known as latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA), which identified 17 topics grouped into four areas: agricultural systems, climate change adaptation, climate change mitigation and enabling environments. Taken together, these findings show that social capital is reflected in research experiences, but its relationship with scaling pathways is not systematically documented, making it more difficult to compare experiences and support institutional learning. One example documents the dissemination of drought-tolerant bean varieties to more than 10, 000 farmers across three countries through sustained partnerships among national agricultural research organizations, seed companies and farmer associations.

Another example describes how collaboration between researchers and governments contributed to the inclusion of agroclimatic information in the nationally determined contributions (NDCs) of several countries under the Paris Agreement.
