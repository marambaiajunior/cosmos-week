In a recent study of infrared light collected by the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), a group of astronomers detected ice for the first time around the young star Beta Pictoris.

The study, published in Science, found that the ice is mostly noncrystalline and mixed with carbon dioxide. Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Add to Preferred Sources Science (2026).

Reflectance spectra of the β Pictoris disk. In the new study, the astronomers used JWST's near-infrared spectrograph (NIRSpec) to map the disk and analyze reflected starlight from opposite sides of the star.

The spectra also showed that carbon dioxide was embedded in the water ice, with roughly one carbon dioxide molecule for every 10 water molecules. The study authors write, "We therefore suggest that a CO₂-H₂O mixture is probably the dominant configuration of the CO₂ ice, with the CO₂ ice located in a H₂O-rich environment in the β Pictoris disk.
