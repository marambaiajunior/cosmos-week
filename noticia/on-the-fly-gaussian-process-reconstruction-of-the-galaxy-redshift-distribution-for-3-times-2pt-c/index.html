The joint analysis of weak gravitational lensing, photometric galaxy clustering, and their cross-correlation, the so-called 3$\times$2pt analysis, is one of the primary probes Stage-IV surveys will use to constrain the dark-energy equation of state.

Its accuracy depends critically on the calibration of the underlying photometric redshift distributions, $n(z)$, whose residual uncertainties can bias cosmological inference. However, conventional parametric nuisance models may not adequately capture the full range of plausible $n(z)$ uncertainties.

We therefore present a cosmological inference framework that instead reconstructs $n(z)$ on the fly using Gaussian Processes (GPs), replacing per-bin shift-and-width parameterisations by a non-parametric, data-driven model. We validate the method on synthetic Euclid-like 3$\times$2pt data vectors representative of Euclid Data Release 1, sampling GP hyperparameters jointly with cosmological and nuisance parameters to reliably propagate $n(z)$ uncertainties into the posterior distributions.

We find that the dependence of the inferred parameters on the adopted $n(z)$ model is markedly stronger in $w_0w_a$CDM than in $Λ$CDM. Opening the dark-energy sector both amplifies the biases induced by a given $n(z)$ treatment and changes which $n(z)$ reconstruction the data prefer, so that a model validated in $Λ$CDM does not automatically remain adequate once $w_0$ and $w_a$ are free.

Our results thus indicate that Stage-IV surveys should define $n(z)$ uncertainty marginalisation requirements separately in cosmologies beyond $Λ$CDM to ensure unbiased dark-energy constraints.
