The formation and evolution of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) remain major open questions in astrophysics. Astrophysical models of SMBHs invoke black-hole (BH) seeds formed at high redshift, which subsequently grow through gas accretion and mergers.

Although observations of high-redshift quasars and active galactic nuclei constrain these scenarios, the mechanisms governing SMBH formation and growth remain uncertain. Future gravitational-wave (GW) observations across the millihertz, decihertz, and audio-frequency bands will provide a complementary and direct probe of SMBH assembly by tracing their merger history.

We investigate the extent to which multiband GW observations can reconstruct the mass-redshift distribution of these mergers. We simulate populations of BH mergers predicted by different astrophysical models and identify those observable by the millihertz detector LISA, the decihertz detector LGWA, and next-generation ground-based detectors operating in the audio-frequency band.

We reconstruct the mass-redshift distribution of these mergers using the nonparametric inference code FIGARO. We find that combining observations across frequency bands substantially improves the reconstruction for the light-seed models considered here, whose merger populations span broad ranges of mass and redshift.

In contrast, for the heavy-seed models considered here, LISA alone recovers most of the accessible mass-redshift distribution, with little additional improvement from the multiband network.
