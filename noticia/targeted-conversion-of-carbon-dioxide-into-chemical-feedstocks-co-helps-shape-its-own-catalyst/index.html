Electrochemists at the University of Duisburg-Essen have discovered through computer simulations that carbon dioxide (CO₂) helps shape the surface of its own catalyst when the catalyst is restructured using voltage pulses.

The findings have been published in Advanced Functional Materials. This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies.

Carbon dioxide, a climate-damaging waste gas from industrial processes, could serve as a valuable raw material. Using electricity, it can be converted into chemical feedstocks that are currently often produced from crude oil or natural gas.

Exner from the University of Duisburg-Essen (UDE) have investigated how these processes can be controlled. The restructuring takes place in two steps: a positive voltage pulse, in combination with water, causes individual titanium atoms to protrude slightly from the surface.

A second voltage pulse with reversed polarity triggers further restructuring, but only if CO₂ itself is involved. This creates a new reaction site that yields a different mix of products.

Pablo Lozano‐Reis et al, Electrochemical Potential Pulses Steer the CO 2 Reduction Reaction Selectivity on Single‐Atom Centers of Ti 3 C 2 T x MXene by Surface Reconstruction, Advanced Functional Materials (2026).
