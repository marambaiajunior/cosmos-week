It sees more than 100 times as much of the sky in one image as the Hubble or James Webb Space Telescope," explains Leiden astronomer Koen Kuijken. It produces around 20 new images every day.

Over its six-year mission, it will map a third of the entire sky. " Its first major data set, released now, already contains an astonishing amount of information: images covering an area of sky almost 10, 000 times the size of the full moon, around 900 million galaxies and 60. Some of the galaxies in the data set existed when the universe was only 700 million years old, just 5% of its current age.

We can describe the history of the universe remarkably well, but on the other hand we still do not understand what 95% of it is made of," says Leiden professor Alessandra Silvestri. And Euclid, alias our dark universe detective, is designed especially with this goal in mind, to help us unravel them. " Everything we can see, from stars and planets to galaxies, accounts for only about 5% of its total contents.

Euclid is revolutionizing the way we study our universe, providing unprecedented insights and setting a new standard for cosmology," says Silvestri.
