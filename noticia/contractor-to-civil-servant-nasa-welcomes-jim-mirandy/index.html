Mirandy is now a NASA civil servant at Stennis as part of the administrator’s directive to strengthen technical core competencies within the civil service workforce. NASA/Danny Nowlin The rumble of a rocket engine might be considered a soundtrack for Jim Mirandy’s life.

Hired under the agency’s workforce directive to restore core competencies by converting contractors to civil service, Mirandy is now a quality assurance specialist after 30 years of working as a contractor supporting NASA. As a nation, we lead the world in space exploration, and we make that dream of reaching for the stars a reality. ” His work at the nation’s largest rocket propulsion test site directly supports the agency’s mission to return American astronauts to the Moon through the Artemis.

By retaining Mirandy’s facility-specific expertise as a civil servant, NASA ensures that the critical propulsion testing at NASA Stennis continues to be executed with safety and quality in mind. Mirandy also has a long resume of critical institutional contributions, including creating high pressure and cryogenic training classes that he taught for 10 years, and helping establish safety protocols throughout various locations at the center.

His father, Wes Mirandy, worked for Rocketdyne at NASA’s Kennedy Space Center in Florida, and Mirandy’s nearby elementary school regularly gathered to watch launches.
