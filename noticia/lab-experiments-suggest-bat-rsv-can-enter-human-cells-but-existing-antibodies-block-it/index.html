Their findings have now been published in the journal Science Advances. The team examined 1, 778 bats from Ghana and Benin.

The epidemiological data showed a low virus detection rate of 1.4%. Antibodies against the virus were detected in 9% of the studied bats.

A detailed analysis of the RSV genomes from two bats showed that the bat viruses are not closely related to human RSV. Bat RSV can inhibit the human innate immune response, the body's built-in first line of defense, but it is neutralized by antibodies from individuals who have previously been exposed to RSV.
