Relativistic jets are produced by accreting black holes across a wide range of masses, from stellar-mass X-ray binaries to supermassive active galactic nuclei (AGN).

In low-luminosity AGN, these jets are believed to be powered by strongly magnetised accretion flows, a picture supported by Event Horizon Telescope and VLBI observations of M87. The jets often exhibit high-energy flaring emission which, if partly hadronic, implies that baryons must be loaded into the jet and accelerated despite strong magnetic barriers.

However, the mechanism behind this remains unclear. We present high-resolution, three-dimensional General Relativistic Magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) simulations of a high-spin Magnetically Arrested Disk (MAD), a strongly magnetised accretion regime.

Using passive Eulerian tracers, we track the exchange of material between the jet and disk to quantify mixing. We find that magnetic flux eruptions, a hallmark of MAD accretion, expel magnetic flux tubes initially filled with jet material (expected to be pair-dominated in real systems).

Through magnetic reconnection during these eruptions, this material is mixed into the disk. Crucially, the same flux tubes reconnect the disk back to the jet, allowing disk material, expected to be primarily hadronic, to infiltrate the jet sheath and boundary.

This disk-to-jet injection offers a natural mass-loading mechanism that does not require the jet to be globally baryon-dominated.
