We present a systematic comparison of stellar spectral libraries and modelling approaches for computing facular and spot contamination spectra across the full Ariel wavelength range (0.5-7.8 microns) for six stellar spectral types spanning F3 to M2. We find that library-dependent differences in the facular treatment introduce systematic biases exceeding the Ariel Tier 3 sensitivity threshold of approximately 20 ppm across most of the wavelength range, for most spectral types and activity levels considered.

These biases align with the absorption bands of water, carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, and methane, rendering them indistinguishable from genuine atmospheric signals at Ariel's precision. Our results demonstrate hat the choice of stellar spectral library, in particular the faculae treatment is a critical and currently underappreciated source of systematic error for Ariel atmospheric characterisation.

The impact on atmospheric retrievals is quantified in a companion paper.
