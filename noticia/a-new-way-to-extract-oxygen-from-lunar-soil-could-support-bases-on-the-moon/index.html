Texto completo

Computer chip manufacturing and lunar exploration seem like completely different worlds. Yet our latest research suggests the two may be more closely connected than expected.

Vijayaraghavan, Aidan Cowley, The Conversation This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies. The work is published in the journal Scientific Reports.

But transporting large quantities from Earth would be enormously expensive, so space agencies are investigating ways to obtain it directly on the moon instead. Instead, it is chemically bound within minerals that make up the moon's surface layer.

To investigate, we exposed artificial moon soil (known as lunar regolith simulants) to plasmas generated using gases that are commonly employed in semiconductor manufacturing. During plasma treatment of the lunar regolith simulants, we detected oxygen and oxygen-containing gaseous products being released.

Further analyses confirmed that the plasma was chemically modifying oxygen-bearing minerals within the material, with some lunar minerals reacting more readily than others. Space engineering often requires entirely new technologies, but there are advantages in adapting systems that already exist, are well understood and have been refined for years on Earth.

Aneeta Jaggernauth et al, Impact of oxygen extraction from lunar regolith simulant using fluorinated plasma processes, Scientific Reports (2026).
