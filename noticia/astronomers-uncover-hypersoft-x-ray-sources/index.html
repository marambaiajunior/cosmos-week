However, according to a new paper by Mustafa Muhibullah and Jimmy Irwin from the University of Alabama and Rosanne Di Stefano from the Center for Astrophysics at Harvard & the Smithsonian, there appears to be another group of ultra-bright X-ray emitters that we’ve completely. Right below them in the energy range are the Extreme Ultra-Violet (EUV) photons, which range between 13.6 eV and 124 eV.

When looking at data from NASA’s Chandra X-Ray Observatory, which has been in operation for more than 20 years, the team set a series of criteria that they define as characteristics of HSSs - they must be observable between 150 and 300 eV, with basically no emission above that. When they looked at six nearby galaxies, including Andromeda and the Pinwheel Galaxy, the team found 84 distinct HSSs - an extremely high number of extremely bright objects.
