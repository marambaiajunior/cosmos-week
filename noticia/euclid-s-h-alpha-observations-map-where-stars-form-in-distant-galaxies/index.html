The space telescope's first major data release covers some 1, 900 square degrees of the sky, equivalent to around 14% of the area planned for the six-year mission and around 5% of the total sky. The data include approximately 10, 000 exceptionally sharp images obtained by the satellite's VIS (Visible instrument) and NISP (Near-Infrared Spectrometer and Photometer), besides more than 160 million spectra.

Louis Gabarra, from Oxford's Department of Physics, is lead author of a new study which used data from Euclid's NISP to obtain detailed information about how and where stars form within galaxies, providing new insights into galaxy evolution. The work is published on the arXiv preprint server.
