A research team from the Institut national de la recherche scientifique (INRS), led by professor Jinyang Liang, is pushing the limits of ultrafast imaging with a new technology capable of capturing three-dimensional images at speeds of up to 150, 000 frames per second.

Published in the journal Nature Communications, this study establishes a new world record in profilometry. This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies.

Credit: Jinyang Liang (INRS) A research team from the Institut national de la recherche scientifique (INRS), led by professor Jinyang Liang, is pushing the limits of ultrafast imaging with a new technology capable of capturing three-dimensional images at speeds of up to 150, 000.

Using C-BLIP, the researchers successfully captured and measured a variety of transient phenomena, including the collision between a projectile and a turbofan blade, the aerodynamics of a falling maple samara and the vibrations of a loudspeaker membrane. This capability opens new possibilities for studying dynamic phenomena that were previously difficult to observe and measure.

Current ultrafast 3D imaging systems are often limited by acquisition speed, the computational complexity of image reconstruction or the high cost of specialized equipment.
