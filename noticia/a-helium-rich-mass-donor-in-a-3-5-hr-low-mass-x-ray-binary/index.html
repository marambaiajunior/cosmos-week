Abstract: Binary systems in which a neutron star accretes material from a low-mass stellar companion arise from evolutionary processes that depend sensitively on the progenitor properties. It is usually assumed that the accreted material has approximately solar composition, unless the orbital period is $\lesssim80$ min, indicating an "ultracompact" system with a hydrogen deficient donor.

However, few constraints on the donor composition are available. Here we apply a Bayesian model-comparison framework, BEANSP, to a sample of 21 thermonuclear bursts from the accretion-powered millisecond pulsar, IGR J17511-3057, to constrain the fuel composition as well as the system parameters.

Our analysis strongly indicates an extremely hydrogen- and CNO-deficient fuel composition, with $X \approx 0.03$ and $Z_\rm{CNO}\approx 4 \times 10^{-4}$, unexpected for this system with its relatively long 3.5 hr orbital period. We explore the possible circumstances under which such an object can evolve, and discuss the implications for its history also in light of the three transient outbursts that have been observed to date.

Such analyses are finding an unexpectedly wide range in donor compositions, that may help to explain the remarkable diversity in the burst properties of the known burst sources.
