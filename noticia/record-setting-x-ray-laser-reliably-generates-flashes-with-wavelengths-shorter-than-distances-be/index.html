Researchers from DESY and European XFEL have demonstrated reliable production and transport of X-ray laser flashes with a wavelength of about 0.04 nanometers (or a photon energy of 30, 000 electronvolts) at European XFEL, raising their own world record.

This means that the X-ray's wavelength is much shorter than typical distances between neighboring atoms in solids, which enables the study of materials with higher spatial resolution than before. Published in Optica, the work could help scientists investigate dense materials and follow rapid changes in experiments that subject samples to extreme pressures, which is relevant for investigating functional, catalytic and quantum materials.

The team generated 800 flashes per second and transported them along a roughly one-kilometer-long (0.6-mile-long) beamline, with almost 60% of the extreme X-ray light being retained after passing through the steering optics.
