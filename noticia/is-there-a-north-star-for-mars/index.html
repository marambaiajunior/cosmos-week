Is there a North Star for Mars. A guide star for future explorers.

But Martian stargazers wouldn't lack for things to see. Image via Spirit Mars rover on May 19, 2005/ NASA.

Earth’s spin is what defines the length of our day of approximately 24 hours. The nearest visible star to the south celestial pole of Earth is about 9 degrees away.

Artist’s illustration of the relative tilts of the orbits of Earth and Mars, Earth doesn’t orbit exactly upright with respect to our orbit around the sun. Instead, Earth’s tilt is about 23 1/2 degrees.

Meanwhile, Mars’ tilt is about 25 degrees. In the northern sky as seen from Mars, the best candidate for a North Star is located on Mars’ sky dome about 1/2 degree from Mars’ north celestial pole.

Well, while Polaris is relatively bright (47th brightest of all stars in the night sky), the star near Mars’ north celestial pole is faint.
