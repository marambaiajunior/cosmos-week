A China, Italy joint research team has reconstructed the time-resolved spectrum of GRB 221009A, the brightest gamma-ray burst (GRB) ever observed, and proposed that the burst may have produced a magnetically dominated jet with a hollow core.

CSES-01 is a Chinese-Italian mission supported by the China National Space Administration and the Italian Space Agency. Evidence of GRB 221009A, about 2.4 billion light-years away, reached Earth on Oct.

A dynamic Monte Carlo correction, together with cross-calibration against GECAM-C and Fermi observations, enabled the team to recover the intrinsic spectrum and fill the gap at the burst's brightest phase. Based on the observations, the researchers identified an unusual spectral bump at about 2 MeV and a narrow emission line whose energy decreased over time from an initial value of about 40 MeV.

If interpreted as thermal emission, it accounts for about 6% of the gamma-ray energy in that period, suggesting the presence of a relatively weak hot fireball alongside a more powerful, likely magnetized jet. The narrow line, possibly from electron, positron annihilation, was first identified at about 12 MeV with Fermi.

At an earlier stage of the burst, CSES-01 detected the line at about 40 MeV. The researchers proposed a structured jet with a ring-like magnetic field: the weak line emission near the axis arrived first, while stronger off-axis emission produced the observed rise-then-fall pattern.

HEPP-H let us study the burst when it was exceptionally bright and reveal an additional broad component at ~2 MeV that had not been observed in this phase," said Li Xinqiao of IHEP, who is also the corresponding author of the study.
