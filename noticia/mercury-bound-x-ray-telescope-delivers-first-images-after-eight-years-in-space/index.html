Mercury-bound X-ray telescope delivers first images after eight years in space

The first data from a U. K. -built instrument heading to Mercury have provided observations of one of the brightest X-ray sources in the sky from aboard the ESA/JAXA BepiColombo.

Although they have been monitoring the instrument during its trip, it was only in September, following the separation of the Mercury Transfer Module (MTM) from the joined science orbiters. By observing it, the team can work out the performance characteristics of MIXS in space and compare these with calibration measurements made with the same instrument in the laboratory in 2015.

The main observation sequence consisted of 55 spacecraft slews, with 39 hours of observations during Sept. They found this to be slightly better than when it was last measured in the lab in 2015.

Born from Leicester's expertise in X-ray astronomy, MIXS is the first imaging X-ray telescope operating on a planetary science mission, and it will work with other instruments to produce a global map of Mercury's surface atomic composition in unprecedented detail.
