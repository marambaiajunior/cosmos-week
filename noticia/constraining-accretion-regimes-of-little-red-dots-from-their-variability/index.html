Texto completo

The ``little red dots'' are photometrically and spectroscopically non-variable on rest-frame baselines of months, with a $5.9σ$ deficit relative to ordinary active galactic nuclei. The ``little red dots'' (LRDs) are photometrically and spectroscopically non-variable on rest-frame baselines of months, with a $5.9σ$ deficit relative to ordinary active galactic nuclei (AGNs).

If we consider LRDs to be accreting black holes, this non-varying could be a clue about the nature of the accretion. Here, we present a general semi-analytic model of variability from super-Eddington (slim) accretion disks.

We consider three mechanisms that play crucial roles in the observed variability, viz, advective attenuation, photospheric averaging, and photon diffusion filter. With this, above $\mdot \sim$ a few, the fractional rms collapses steeply with $\mdot$.

Comparing with JWST monitoring constraints, we find that the non-detection of LRD variability requires $\mdot \gtrsim 30$ for $M_{\rm BH}\sim10^{7}\, M_\odot$ on a 0.5\, yr rest-frame baseline. A Monte Carlo forward model of the TWINKLE non-detections of 18 LRDs sharpens this to $\mdot\gtrsim60$ at 95\% confidence.

On current baselines, these non-detections remain formally degenerate with a frozen sub-Eddington disk ($\mdot\lesssim0.1$). Longer monitoring will separate the two regimes as the frozen-disk variability grows with baseline, while the super-Eddington collapse does not.

The same framework predicts strongly suppressed X-ray variability, testable with future X-ray observatories.
