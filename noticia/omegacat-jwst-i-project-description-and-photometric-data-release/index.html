Texto completo

Omega Centauri is the most massive globular cluster in the Milky Way and the likely stripped nucleus of an accreted dwarf galaxy. Omega Centauri (NGC 5139, $ω$ Cen) is the most massive globular cluster in the Milky Way and the likely stripped nucleus of an accreted dwarf galaxy.

We present oMEGACat-JWST, a deep photometric and astrometric catalog of $ω$ Cen's core and surroundings that extends our previous oMEGACat efforts to near-infrared wavelengths. The superior sensitivity and angular resolution of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) provide significant astrometric and photometric improvements, particularly for faint stars.

Our catalog is based on a custom reduction of NIRCam and NIRISS imaging across three JWST programs extending out to two half-light radii (10'). In total, our catalog contains approximately 677 000 sources.

In the region overlapping with oMEGACat, we achieve high relative completeness (> 90% of HST sources recovered) while detecting over 86 000 new sources below HST's detection limit. Astrometric tests demonstrate a single-image precision as good as 0.5% of a NIRCam pixel (0.15 mas) at mF115W=18.

At mF115W=23 the precision is still 1 mas. Color-magnitude diagrams based on our data show the well-known split between the primordial Helium and the Helium enriched populations and are particularly sensitive to light-element (CNO) variations of faint stars.

OMEGACat-JWST provides the most complete photometric catalog of $ω$ Cen's core to date.
