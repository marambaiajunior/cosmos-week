Have an idea for a project that will add value for arXiv's community? . Abstract: We train a spherical graph convolutional network to infer the tensor-to-scalar ratio $r$ directly from multifrequency cosmic microwave background (CMB) $B$-mode maps on the cut sky.

We test the network on simulations of a generic ground-based experiment including lensed CMB, inhomogeneous noise, and stochastic Galactic dust and synchrotron emission. We compare the network with a multifrequency bandpower likelihood built from the same 20, 000 simulations used to train it.

Working directly on the maps, with no power-spectrum estimation or component separation, the network reduces the root-mean-square error on $r$ by a factor of 1.3 and gives well-calibrated 68% intervals 1.2 times narrower than the likelihood with the same prior. Saliency maps show that the network learns to favor the deepest regions of the sky and the cleanest frequency channels without being given the survey depth.

Both approaches are tied to the foreground model they are built on: the network generalizes to some alternative foreground models without retraining, but not to a three-dimensional dust model with line-of-sight variations.
