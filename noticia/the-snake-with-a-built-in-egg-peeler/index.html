From the Harenna Forest, Ethiopia (ZSM 94/2019). Arthur Tiutenko from FAU and colleagues from LMU München and the South African National Museum in Bloemfontein discovered a new species of snake in the Harenna Forest, on the southern slopes of Ethiopia's Bale Mountains: Dasypeltis albigularis, or the white-throated egg-eater.

The forest covers a vast area of mountainous territory in the south of Ethiopia and falls steeply from peaks reaching up to 4, 000 meters (13, 100 feet) above sea level to the dry savanna plains at approximately 1, 000 meters (3, 280 feet) above sea level. This region that is considered the birthplace of coffee is still largely considered a blank spot on the map when it comes to science.

One important clue came when this animal mated with a male montane egg-eater: While normally all offspring would hatch in a normal clutch of eggs, when these animals crossbred, only a single female hatched from a total of 21 eggs.

The FAU researcher's suspicion was finally confirmed by examining the genetic material: The black snake with the white throat from the Harenna Forest is obviously a separate, previously undiscovered species.
