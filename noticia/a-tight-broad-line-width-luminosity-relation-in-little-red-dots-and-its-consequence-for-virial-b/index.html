Texto completo

JWST has uncovered compact high-redshift sources with red, $\sim 5000$ K optical continua and broad Balmer lines, known as "little red dots". JWST has uncovered compact high-redshift sources with red, $\sim 5000$ K optical continua and broad Balmer lines, known as "little red dots" (LRDs).

Whether these lines trace gas orbiting a massive black hole or electron scattering in a dense, Eddington-limited envelope around a lighter black hole leaves inferred masses spanning $10^{4}$--$10^{8}\, M_\odot$. We test both pictures by uniformly refitting public JWST/NIRSpec spectra of 29 LRDs, 15 other JWST broad-line sources, and luminosity-matched SDSS quasars.

At fixed $\mathrm{H}α$ luminosity, LRD broad $\mathrm{H}α$ widths are remarkably uniform: their intrinsic scatter is 0.054 dex ($<0.075$ dex at 95%), less than half that of quasars (0.12 dex), clustering near $1760\ \mathrm{km\, s^{-1}}$ outside the three brightest sources.

Because single-epoch virial masses scale as width squared, they scatter intrinsically by $<0.16$ dex at fixed $\mathrm{H}α$ luminosity, making virial masses and mass functions nearly deterministic restatements of luminosity regardless of the underlying physics.

Bound orbits outside a spherical photosphere thus require sub-Eddington luminosities ($\leq 0.10\text{--}0.30\, L_{\rm Edd}$), whereas an Eddington or super-Eddington envelope requires uniform $2\text{--}3\times$ non-gravitational broadening within the $\mathrm{H}α$-emitting gas.
