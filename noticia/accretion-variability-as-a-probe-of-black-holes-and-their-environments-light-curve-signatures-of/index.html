Accretion variability as a probe of black holes and their environments: Light curve signatures of a single hot spot

The compact radio source at the centre of the Milky Way, Sagittarius A*, exhibits temporal broadband brightness variability.

Texto completo

The compact radio source at the centre of the Milky Way, Sagittarius A*, exhibits temporal broadband brightness variability. The observed timescales of such fluctuations, ranging from minutes to hours on horizon scales, arise from the complex interplay of gravitation, plasma, and light propagation processes in the immediate vicinity of the supermassive black hole.

Kinetic and fluid simulations associate this variability with turbulence and the formation of magnetised, localised plasma structures. Focusing on a single fluctuating compact emission region, a 'hot spot', we study how intrinsic source fluctuations map onto different regions of the observer's screen.

Our aim is to identify distinct imprints of light propagation effects, which encode information both about the source and the underlying space-time geometry. We simulate image time series by propagating the emitted light to a distant observer's sky plane in Minkowski, Schwarzschild, and Kerr space-times.

We then compute the corresponding observed light curves and power spectra, both for the full observed images and for selected angular sectors of the observer's sky plane. We derive a semi-analytical model for the power spectrum of a fluctuating hot spot in flat space-time, reproducing all qualitative features of curved-space-time spectra at moderate inclinations.

Moreover, we describe how such power spectra are affected by space-time geometry, inclination, and source velocity, and show that orthogonal two-sector angular decompositions of the sky plane provide information on system orientation and lensing effects. The physical effects highlighted in this paper are expected to be present in the timing signature of accretion-flow variability, as observed by current instruments and future photometric and interferometric facilities.

Further work is needed to investigate if our conclusions hold for realistic disc configurations.
