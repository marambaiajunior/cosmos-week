This matters because chemistry gains force when a claimed structure or process can be described with enough precision to be reproduced by others. Synthetic routes, spectroscopic signatures, yield under defined conditions and stability under realistic operating parameters are the currency of credibility in chemistry, and a result that lacks these details cannot be evaluated independently. The distance between a discovery on a laboratory bench and a process that works reliably at scale is measured in years of optimization, and each step reveals constraints that were invisible at smaller scale.
